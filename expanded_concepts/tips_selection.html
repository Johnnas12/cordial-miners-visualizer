What does this example demonstrate?

  1. Local visibility: With four validators and one tolerated fault, three distinct validators meet the acknowledgement threshold. Miner 3 could build a Round 2 candidate from M2·R1, M3·R1, and M4·R1 before receiving M1·R1. A scheduler still decides when to propose.
  2. Paper versus Rust: The paper selects maximal tips from the completed-round prefix of the local blocklace. Our Rust selector takes the latest block from each bonded, non-equivocating validator, even when another validator's block already references it. After M1·R2, only Round 1 is complete, so the paper's depth-1 prefix selects Round 1 blocks while Rust selects M1·R2.
  3. Equivocation: This scene has only honest chains. In Rust strict mode, known equivocators are omitted; the default compatibility mode may also reference unseen conflicting branches to retain evidence. Those cases are outside this three-step example.
